Construction--Barrier

Definition: A physical obstruction designed to block or control passage, separate areas, or provide protection. This is a general category for any barrier.
Visual Characteristics: Can be linear or block-like. Made from various materials (concrete, metal, wood, plastic). Its primary function is to impede movement or delineate space.
Examples: Guardrails, fences, concrete blocks, walls, road medians.

Definition: A barrier whose specific type or function is not clearly identifiable from the visual information provided, or which could fall under multiple specific barrier subcategories due to limited visibility or atypical design.
Visual Characteristics: An identifiable physical obstruction, but details like material, precise shape, or clear purpose are obscured, too far away, or simply non-standard. It's clearly a barrier, but its exact nature is uncertain.
Examples: A blurry or partially obscured barrier, a makeshift barrier, a barrier that combines features of several types.
construction--barrier--concrete-block

Definition: A barrier composed of distinct, solid blocks of concrete, typically large and heavy, used to delineate or protect areas.
Visual Characteristics: Segmented, grey or weathered concrete units. Often rectangular or L-shaped. Can be arranged in a continuous line or as individual obstacles. Heaviness is implied by material and mass.
Examples: Jersey barriers, large concrete planters used as barriers, individual concrete cubes blocking an entrance.
construction--barrier--curb

Definition: A raised edge typically made of concrete or stone, forming a boundary between a road and a sidewalk or median.
Visual Characteristics: A distinct, elevated edge, usually grey, white, or textured, separating two ground surfaces of different elevations. Often continuous along a road edge.
Examples: Standard street curbs, curbs around traffic islands.
construction--barrier--fence

Definition: A barrier constructed from posts and wire, wood, or other thin materials, typically permeable to light and air, used to enclose or delineate an area.
Visual Characteristics: Composed of vertical posts and horizontal elements (e.g., wire mesh, wooden planks, chain-link). Often has gaps or openings allowing visibility through it. Varies in height and material.
Examples: Chain-link fence, wooden picket fence, privacy fence, temporary construction fence.
construction--barrier--guard-rail

Definition: A safety barrier, typically made of metal (e.g., W-beam), designed to prevent vehicles from leaving the road or crossing into opposing traffic.
Visual Characteristics: A long, continuous metal barrier, often wavy or corrugated, supported by vertical posts. Usually silver/grey, sometimes painted. Found alongside roads, on bridges, or separating lanes.
Examples: Highway guardrails, crash barriers.
construction--barrier--other-barrier

Definition: A barrier that does not fit into the more specific subcategories listed (e.g., concrete-block, curb, fence, guard-rail, road-median, road-side, separator, temporary, wall).
Visual Characteristics: Diverse. Could be a line of bollards, a row of large rocks, water-filled plastic barriers, or any other physical impediment not explicitly covered.
Examples: Bollards, decorative planters used as barriers, sandbags, hay bales.
construction--barrier--road-median

Definition: A divided section of a road that separates opposing lanes of traffic, often featuring vegetation, concrete, or other structures. It acts as a permanent barrier between traffic flows.
Visual Characteristics: A strip, often raised, in the center of a road separating directions of travel. Can contain grass, trees, concrete, or other physical barriers. Typically continuous for a significant length.
Examples: Grass median on a highway, concrete barrier median, landscaped median in a city street.
construction--barrier--road-side

Definition: A barrier located directly adjacent to the side of a road, serving to contain vehicles or separate the road from adjacent terrain or property. This can encompass guardrails, walls, or other structures along the edge.
Visual Characteristics: A linear structure running parallel to the road's edge, clearly intended to separate the road from what lies beyond (e.g., a drop-off, private property, a pedestrian area). It's distinct from a central median.
Examples: A guardrail along a cliff edge, a retaining wall bordering a road, a continuous fence alongside a highway.
construction--barrier--separator

Definition: A physical barrier used to divide specific lanes or areas of traffic within the same direction of travel, often for dedicated lanes (e.g., bike lanes, bus lanes) or to guide traffic flow. Differs from a road-median by not necessarily separating opposing traffic.
Visual Characteristics: Smaller than a typical median, often low-profile, concrete, plastic, or rubber structures. They are usually designed to be hit without severe damage to the vehicle, yet effectively deter crossing.
Examples: Flexible lane delineators, concrete islands separating turn lanes, small curbed islands between a car lane and a bike lane.
construction--barrier--temporary

Definition: A barrier that is not a permanent fixture, often used for construction zones, event management, or short-term traffic control.
Visual Characteristics: Often brightly colored (orange, white), made of plastic, cones, or lightweight metal. Easily movable. Often arranged in sequences to guide or block.
Examples: Orange traffic cones, plastic water-filled barriers, temporary fencing around a construction site.
construction--barrier--wall

Definition: A solid, continuous, and typically tall barrier made of stone, brick, concrete, or similar robust materials, designed for enclosure, support, or protection.
Visual Characteristics: A vertical, opaque structure, generally higher than a curb or fence. Often made of masonry or solid concrete. Forms a significant physical boundary.
Examples: Garden wall, sound barrier wall along a highway, building exterior wall, retaining wall.
